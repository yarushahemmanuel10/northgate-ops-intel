# Suspected compromised account

> Synthetic document. Northgate IT Services Ltd is a fictional company; this text was written for a portfolio prototype.

## Signs of a compromised account

- Customers or colleagues receive strange emails from the user's address.
- The user sees sign-in alerts from places they have never been.
- Inbox rules appear that the user did not create, often forwarding or deleting email.
- The user receives multi-factor prompts they did not trigger.

## Act quickly

Treat a confirmed compromise as a P1 if the account has access to finance systems or customer data, and a P2 otherwise. Work through these steps in order without waiting for the end of the call.

1. Block sign-in for the account and revoke all active sessions.
2. Reset the password. Give the new temporary password to the user by phone.
3. Check the user's authentication methods and remove any the user does not recognise.
4. Review inbox rules and forwarding settings and remove any malicious ones.
5. Review the sign-in and audit logs to see what the attacker accessed and whether email or files were exported.
6. Unblock sign-in once the account is secure, and make sure multi-factor authentication is enforced.

## Tell the right people

Tell the customer's main contact what happened. If personal data may have been accessed, the customer may need to report it; point them to our data handling policy and offer to help with the facts they will need.

## After the incident

Check whether the same phishing email reached other users, and record what was found on the ticket.
