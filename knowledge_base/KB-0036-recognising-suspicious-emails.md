# Recognising suspicious emails

> Synthetic document. Northgate IT Services Ltd is a fictional company; this text was written for a portfolio prototype.

## Why it matters

Most attacks on small businesses start with an email. A convincing message asks you to click a link, open an attachment, or pay an invoice. Spotting these emails protects you and your colleagues.

## Warning signs

- The message creates urgency: an account about to close, an overdue invoice, a parcel waiting.
- The sender's address does not match the organisation it claims to be from, even if the display name does.
- You are asked to sign in through a link, confirm your password, or approve a payment.
- An attachment you were not expecting, especially one that asks you to enable content.
- A message from a colleague or supplier asking you to change bank details for a payment.

## What to do

1. Do not click links, open attachments or reply.
2. Use the report button in your email client to report the message.
3. Do not forward the email to the service desk or to colleagues.
4. If you have already clicked a link or typed your password, phone the service desk straight away. The sooner we know, the less damage an attacker can do.

## Payment requests

Always check a request to change bank details by phoning the supplier on a number you already have, never one given in the email.

## If your account is misused

If colleagues or customers tell you they have received odd emails from you, phone the service desk: your account may have been compromised.
